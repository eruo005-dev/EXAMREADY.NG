JAMB Mathematics is 40 questions in 120 minutes (3 minutes per question). The good news: about 75% of those questions come from 10 topics that appear every single year. Drill these and you can comfortably target 30+ of 40 marks before considering the harder topics.

This guide covers each topic with a worked example pulled from common past-paper patterns.

## 1. Number bases and modular arithmetic

Almost every JAMB Math paper has 2-3 questions on number bases. The trick: don't memorise — practise the conversion algorithm.

**Worked example:** Convert 110₁₁₀ in base 2 to a base-10 number.

Working: 110₁₁₀ doesn't make sense because 110 is the BASE not a number. Re-read the question. Standard form: convert 11011₂ to base 10.

11011₂ = 1×2⁴ + 1×2³ + 0×2² + 1×2¹ + 1×2⁰ = 16 + 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = **27**

**Drill:** practise base-10 → base-2 → base-8 → base-16 conversions both ways. Spend 30 minutes here once and you'll recognise every JAMB number-base question instantly.

## 2. Algebraic expressions and factorisation

8-10 questions per JAMB paper hit algebra in some form. The patterns:

- Expand and simplify
- Factorise quadratics
- Solve simultaneous equations
- Substitution

**Worked example:** If x² + 5x − 14 = 0, find the values of x.

Working: factorise. Find two numbers that multiply to −14 and add to 5. Those are 7 and −2. So (x + 7)(x − 2) = 0. **x = −7 or x = 2.**

**Drill:** the 12 most common quadratic patterns. Once you've factorised 50 quadratics, you'll spot the pattern in 5 seconds.

## 3. Indices and logarithms

3-4 questions per paper. The laws are short; the application is what trips students up.

**Worked example:** Simplify 2³ × 2⁻⁵ ÷ 2⁻⁴.

Working: when multiplying, add exponents; when dividing, subtract. So 2³ × 2⁻⁵ = 2³⁻⁵ = 2⁻². Then 2⁻² ÷ 2⁻⁴ = 2⁻²⁻(⁻⁴) = 2² = **4**.

**The 4 laws to memorise:**

1. aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
2. aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
3. (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
4. a⁰ = 1, a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ

For logarithms: log_a(xy) = log_a(x) + log_a(y), log_a(x/y) = log_a(x) − log_a(y), log_a(xⁿ) = n·log_a(x). That's it.

## 4. Mensuration (area, volume, surface area)

3-4 questions per paper. Memorise the formulae, practise spotting which one applies.

**Common formulae you'll need:**

- Area of rectangle: length × width
- Area of triangle: ½ × base × height
- Area of circle: πr²
- Surface area of cylinder: 2πrh + 2πr²
- Volume of cylinder: πr²h
- Volume of cone: ⅓πr²h
- Volume of sphere: ⁴⁄₃πr³
- Surface area of sphere: 4πr²

**Worked example:** Find the volume of a cone with radius 7 cm and height 12 cm. Take π = ²²⁄₇.

Working: V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓ × ²²⁄₇ × 49 × 12 = ⅓ × 22 × 7 × 12 = ⅓ × 1848 = **616 cm³**.

## 5. Statistics — mean, median, mode

2-3 questions per paper. Often involve frequency tables.

**Worked example:** The mean of the numbers 4, 6, x, 10, 12 is 8. Find x.

Working: mean = sum / count. (4 + 6 + x + 10 + 12) / 5 = 8. So 32 + x = 40. **x = 8.**

**Drill the patterns:**

- Mean from raw data
- Mean from a frequency table
- Median from a frequency table (find the middle position)
- Mode is the most frequent value
- Range = max − min

## 6. Probability

2-3 questions per paper.

**Worked example:** A bag contains 3 red and 5 blue balls. One ball is drawn at random. What is the probability that it is blue?

Working: P(blue) = number of blue / total = 5/8.

**Drill:** mutually exclusive events (P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)), independent events (P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)), conditional probability for the harder questions.

## 7. Geometry — angles, polygons, circles

3-4 questions per paper.

**Key facts:**

- Angles on a straight line sum to 180°
- Angles in a triangle sum to 180°
- Angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360°
- Angle at the centre of a circle is twice the angle at the circumference (subtended by the same arc)
- Angle in a semicircle is 90°

**Worked example:** Find the value of x if the angles of a triangle are (2x + 10)°, (3x − 20)°, and (x + 30)°.

Working: 2x + 10 + 3x − 20 + x + 30 = 180. So 6x + 20 = 180. 6x = 160. **x = 26.67° (or 80/3)**.

## 8. Trigonometry

2-3 questions per paper. The basic trig ratios + sine and cosine rules.

**Worked example:** If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find cos θ.

Working: use sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. cos²θ = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25. Since θ is acute, cos θ is positive. **cos θ = 4/5.**

## 9. Sets and Venn diagrams

1-2 questions per paper, often visual.

**Worked example:** In a class of 50 students, 30 like Math and 25 like Physics. If 10 like both, how many like neither?

Working: students who like Math OR Physics = 30 + 25 − 10 = 45. Students who like neither = 50 − 45 = **5**.

**Drill:** know the formula |A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B|. Practise translating word problems into Venn diagrams.

## 10. Sequences and series (AP and GP)

1-2 questions per paper.

**AP (arithmetic progression):** common difference d. nth term = a + (n−1)d.
**GP (geometric progression):** common ratio r. nth term = arⁿ⁻¹.

**Worked example:** The first three terms of an AP are 3, 8, 13. Find the 20th term.

Working: a = 3, d = 8 − 3 = 5. 20th term = 3 + (20 − 1) × 5 = 3 + 95 = **98**.

## How to use this list

The fastest way to lift a JAMB Math score from 50% to 75% is to drill these 10 topics until each is automatic. Spend 1 hour per day per topic for 10 days, and you'll have covered 75% of the paper at high accuracy.

The remaining 25% comes from less common topics: calculus, permutations and combinations, matrices, coordinate geometry. They show up but only 1-2 questions per paper. Don't ignore them, but they're lower priority than the 10 above.
