A 300+ JAMB score puts you in roughly the top 5% of candidates and unlocks Medicine, Pharmacy, Law, Engineering at every Nigerian university including the most competitive ones. It's not impossible — but it's not just "study hard" either.

Here are 9 strategies that show up consistently in the study habits of students who scored 300+. None of them require expensive lessons or special access. All of them require honest, structured time.

## 1. Calibrate the bar early. 300 isn't a goal — it's an outcome.

Students who hit 300 don't aim for 300. They aim for 320-340, knowing that nerves + bad-luck questions will cost them 20-40 points. If your aim is 300 exactly, your realistic outcome is 270-290.

**What to do:** assume your real outcome is 80% of your practice average. If you average 75% in mock papers (= 300), you'll likely score 280-290 in the real thing. To hit 300+, target 80%+ in mocks consistently.

## 2. Past papers > textbooks (after foundations).

The first 2 months of preparation should be foundational — work through the syllabus topic-by-topic. After that, **shift 70% of your study time to past papers, especially 2019-2024**. JAMB recycles question patterns aggressively. The same question rarely appears twice, but the same TYPE of question appears every year.

**Concrete habit:** every Saturday morning, do one full mock paper from a recent year. Mark it the same day. Spend Sunday going over wrong answers. Over 6 months that's 24 full mock papers — more than enough exposure to JAMB's patterns.

## 3. Time the questions, not just the paper.

The JAMB UTME is 120 minutes for 160 questions = 45 seconds per question. Most students don't realise that's the constraint until exam day, when they finish English Language but run out of time on Mathematics.

**Habit:** when you practise, set a 30-second timer per question for the first month, then 45 seconds. Build the speed BEFORE you need it. By month 3, 60-second questions should feel slow.

## 4. Master the comprehension shortcut.

English Language is 40 questions including comprehension passages. Most students read the entire passage, then read the questions, then re-read parts of the passage looking for the answers. That's reading the passage 1.5x on average.

**Faster:** read the questions FIRST. Note the question keywords. Then read the passage looking for those keywords. Most passages take 2-3 minutes to read this way vs 5-6 minutes the slow way. **You'll save 10-15 minutes** per English paper.

## 5. Drop the topics that historically appear < 5% of the time.

JAMB's syllabus is broad. Some topics show up every year (algebra, comprehension, mechanics). Some show up 1-2 questions a year. Some haven't shown up in 5 years.

If you've covered every topic and need to choose where to deepen, the data is on your side: spend extra time on **algebra (15+ questions per Math paper), comprehension (20+ per English), mechanics + electricity (12+ per Physics)**, and skip the long tail.

## 6. Study with a study partner — but not for "study sessions".

Group study sessions are mostly social. They don't move scores. What moves scores is the **WhatsApp accountability message at 9pm**: "Did you do your 30 questions today?"

A study partner you message daily is worth 10x a weekly group session. Pick one classmate aiming for a similar score and trade nightly accountability.

## 7. Sleep 8 hours. Every. Single. Night.

This is the single highest-leverage habit and the one students dismiss most. Research is unambiguous: sleep-deprived students score 5-10% lower than well-rested students of equal preparation. **5-10% on JAMB is 20-40 points.**

In the final month, going from 6 hours to 8 hours of sleep moves your score more than studying an extra 2 hours per night.

## 8. Use the AI tutor for SPECIFIC follow-ups, not for general study.

ExamReady's [AI tutor](/) is most valuable when you've answered a question, gotten it wrong, read the explanation, and STILL don't understand. That's the 5% of cases where a back-and-forth with an AI is faster than your textbook.

Don't ask the AI to "teach you algebra". Ask it: "Why is the answer to this specific quadratic question x = -3 instead of x = 3?" The specificity is what makes the response useful.

## 9. Practice on a laptop or desktop at least once a week.

JAMB is computer-based. The exam is administered on desktop computers with mouse and keyboard. If you've spent 6 months practising on your phone, the keyboard layout, the screen size, the cursor latency — all of that will throw off your timing on exam day.

If you don't own a laptop, find a cyber café once a week and do a 30-minute practice session there. The cost is < ₦500. The score difference is meaningful.

## What top scorers don't do

Useful to flip the question:

- They **don't** memorise topics they don't understand. Memorisation breaks under exam pressure.
- They **don't** spend exam day cramming. Final-week cramming reliably hurts performance via stress + sleep loss.
- They **don't** use only one source. Multiple textbooks + multiple past-paper sets + multiple practice platforms expose them to question variants.
- They **don't** over-rely on tricks. Yes, eliminate obviously-wrong options. No, that's not a substitute for actually knowing the material.
- They **don't** discuss the exam with friends DURING the exam window. The temptation to compare answers between sessions is real and the answers are almost always wrong; you'll lose sleep over a question you actually got right.

## A 90-day plan to push from 240 → 300

If you're currently at 240 and aiming for 300, here's a real 90-day plan:

- **Days 1-30**: Diagnose the 30-point gap. Identify which subject is dragging you down. Spend 70% of time there. The other 30% = maintenance on subjects you're already strong in.
- **Days 31-60**: Past papers daily. Mark hard. Keep a missed-marks log. Fix one specific error pattern per week.
- **Days 61-85**: 2 full mocks per week. Aim for 75%+ before the exam.
- **Days 86-90**: Light practice + 8h sleep. Don't introduce anything new.

Realistic outcome: students who execute this plan move 30-50 points on average. Students who do an unstructured 90 days move 10-20.
