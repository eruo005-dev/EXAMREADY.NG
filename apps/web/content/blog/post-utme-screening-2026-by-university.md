Post-UTME is the second gate for Nigerian university admission, and most students under-prepare for it. JAMB is well-known and over-resourced; Post-UTME is the silent killer for students with strong JAMB scores who didn't take screening seriously.

This guide goes university-by-university through the top 10, with the actual screening format and the preparation strategy that works for each.

> **Verify the latest format on each university's official admissions portal before relying on this.** Universities adjust their screening structure year-to-year, especially when they switch between CBT-style and documentation-only modes.

## Universities still running CBT-style screening

These universities require you to physically (or remotely, in some cases) take a screening exam. Plan around the exam.

### University of Ibadan (UI)

- **Format**: 50-minute CBT, 30-50 questions
- **Subjects**: Use of English (50%) + general knowledge / current affairs (50%)
- **Sample questions**: standard JAMB-style English (comprehension, lexis, structure) with current-affairs items mixed in
- **Cut-off**: aggregate of 60-70% (combined with JAMB) for non-Medicine; 75%+ for Medicine
- **Prep**: spend 2-3 weeks on JAMB English-style practice + read Nigerian newspapers daily for current affairs

### University of Nigeria, Nsukka (UNN)

- **Format**: 60-minute CBT, 50-100 questions depending on year
- **Subjects**: Use of English + course-relevant general knowledge
- **Sample questions**: heavier on comprehension than UI; expect 2-3 long passages
- **Cut-off**: aggregate 60%+ for non-Medicine
- **Prep**: comprehension speed-reading is the biggest leverage

### University of Benin (UNIBEN)

- **Format**: 45-minute CBT, 40-50 questions
- **Subjects**: Use of English + general studies questions
- **Sample questions**: simpler English than UI, more general-knowledge weight
- **Cut-off**: aggregate 60-70% by course
- **Prep**: medium-difficulty JAMB English drills + Nigerian-context general knowledge

### Federal University of Technology, Akure (FUTA)

- **Format**: depends on faculty, but most engineering/technology programmes still run a screening test
- **Subjects**: Use of English + relevant subject-specific questions for engineering
- **Cut-off**: aggregate 60%+
- **Prep**: keep JAMB Math + Physics fresh through screening week

### Lagos State University (LASU)

- **Format**: 30-minute CBT or oral interview depending on programme
- **Subjects**: Use of English + Government/Current affairs
- **Cut-off**: aggregate 55-65%
- **Prep**: lighter than UI/UNN; weeks of JAMB English maintenance is enough

## Universities now using documentation/aggregate-only screening

These universities skipped the CBT exam in recent years and admit based on a JAMB + O'Level aggregate. **Strong O'Level grades become the leverage** — A1s in 5+ subjects significantly improve your aggregate.

### University of Lagos (UNILAG)

- **Format**: documentation review; no screening exam in 2023-2024 cycle
- **Aggregate**: 50% JAMB + 50% O'Level grade aggregate
- **What helps**: A1s on O'Level. UNILAG cuts off competitively for Medicine (300+) and Law (290+)
- **Prep**: prioritise getting A1 in your most-relevant 5 subjects; submit your strongest O'Level results (WAEC or NECO, whichever is better)

### Obafemi Awolowo University (OAU)

- **Format**: documentation review only in recent years
- **Aggregate**: 50/50 JAMB and O'Level
- **What helps**: same as UNILAG — strong O'Level

### Ahmadu Bello University (ABU Zaria)

- **Format**: documentation-based
- **Aggregate**: weighted JAMB + O'Level
- **What helps**: strong O'Level + competitive JAMB

### University of Ilorin (UNILORIN)

- **Format**: documentation, with rare CBT for some programmes
- **Aggregate**: heavily weighted toward JAMB but O'Level matters

## How to prepare for documentation-only universities

Since you don't have an exam to study for, your "preparation" looks different:

1. **Make sure your O'Level result upload is clean.** Submit on time, with the correct documentation.
2. **Submit BOTH WAEC and NECO if you have them**, and let the university pick the higher grades. Many candidates lose marks because they only submit one body's results.
3. **Track the application portal daily** — universities sometimes ask for additional documents (NIN, age declaration, indigene certificates) at short notice.
4. **Apply to your indigene state's university if relevant** — quotas matter. State universities admit a higher percentage of indigene candidates, and federal universities also have small indigene quotas.

## The 4-week screening prep plan

If your university runs a CBT screening, here's a tight 4-week plan:

### Week 1: Diagnostic + content review

- One full mock screening test for your university (find past papers from past students; few are public)
- Identify weak areas (English vocab? Comprehension speed? Current affairs?)

### Week 2: English drills

- 1 hour per day on JAMB-style English questions
- Focus on lexis (word meaning) and structure (grammar) — these dominate most screening exams
- Comprehension passages 3 times per week, timed

### Week 3: Current affairs

- Read at least one Nigerian newspaper daily — TheGuardian, Premium Times, Punch
- Note: politics, economy, sports, technology
- For specific universities, the screening sometimes asks about the university itself — its founders, motto, faculty heads

### Week 4: Mock papers + light practice

- 2 full mock papers from past students
- Mark hard
- Sleep 8 hours
