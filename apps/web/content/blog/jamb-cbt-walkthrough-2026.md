The JAMB CBT exam is the same format every year, but if you've never sat one, the interface can throw you off in the first 5 minutes — and 5 minutes is 7 questions. This walkthrough takes you through exactly what happens, in order.

If you can practise the format once on a desktop or laptop before exam day, you'll save real time. ExamReady's [mock CBT mode](/practice?mode=mock_cbt) replicates the exact JAMB UI.

## Before exam day

Confirm:

- [ ] You have your **CBT centre allocation slip** printed (JAMB sends it via the portal)
- [ ] Your **mock exam slip** if you took the JAMB Mock (some centres ask for it as proof)
- [ ] **Photo ID** — your JAMB candidate slip + national ID or driver's licence
- [ ] You know exactly **where the centre is** and how long it takes to get there. Visit it the day before if you've never been.
- [ ] You have **2 sharpened pencils, 2 black biros, an eraser** for the rough paper they'll provide

Get to the centre **90 minutes before** your scheduled session. Late candidates are denied entry.

## Arrival at the centre

Expect:

1. **Biometric check at the gate** — fingerprint scan against the JAMB database. If your fingerprint doesn't match (rare but possible), you're flagged. Bring your candidate slip; have a calm conversation; the centre staff usually have a manual override.
2. **Phone, watch, calculator confiscation** — JAMB doesn't allow ANY electronics in the hall. Leave your phone with someone outside or in the centre's locker. They will check.
3. **Bag drop** — your books, water bottles, and snacks all go in the bag-drop area. You're not allowed to bring food into the hall.
4. **Photo ID verification** at the registration desk. They'll match your face against the photo on your candidate slip.
5. **Hall assignment** — a staff member tells you which computer to sit at. Don't switch seats.

## Login screen

You arrive at your computer. The screen shows the JAMB CBT login. Steps:

1. Type your **JAMB registration number** (10-character code, on your candidate slip)
2. Type your **password** if your centre uses one (most don't — the registration number alone logs you in)
3. Click **Login**

If the system rejects your registration number, **don't panic**. Wave a centre staff over. They'll resolve it manually. This is usually a typo or a computer-side database hiccup.

## Instructions screen

After login, you see a 1-2 page screen of instructions. Read it. Specifically check:

- **Total questions**: should say 160
- **Time allotted**: should say 120 minutes
- **Subjects**: should list your 4 (English + 3 others)
- **Navigation**: how to move between questions
- **Submit**: how to submit at the end

Do NOT click "Start" until you've read this. The clock starts when you click Start.

## The exam interface

You'll see, top-to-bottom:

- **Timer** in the top-right corner. Counts down from 2:00:00. **Watch this.**
- **Subject tabs** — usually English, then your 3 chosen subjects in order. Click to switch between subjects.
- **Question number** prominently displayed (e.g. "Question 1 of 40")
- **Question text** in the middle
- **Options A, B, C, D** as radio buttons below
- **Navigation buttons**:
  - "Previous" — go back one question
  - "Next" — go forward one question
  - "Save & Next" — same as Next on most builds
  - "Question Palette" — jump to any specific question (1-40 grid for the current subject)
- **Submit button** — usually disabled until you click "Finish Exam"

## How to use the time

You have 120 minutes for 160 questions. **Don't spend more than 60 seconds on any first-pass question.** If you can't see the answer in 60 seconds:

1. Note the question number on your scratch paper
2. Click "Save & Next" to skip
3. Come back at the end if time allows

Most students who fail to finish JAMB get stuck on hard Math or comprehension questions early and burn 5-10 minutes that should have gone to questions they could have answered.

**Realistic pacing:**

- Minute 0-30: First pass through English (40 questions, ~45 sec each)
- Minute 30-60: First pass through your strongest subject
- Minute 60-90: First pass through your second-strongest subject
- Minute 90-110: First pass through weakest subject
- Minute 110-120: Return to skipped questions, finalise answers

## Subject switching strategy

You can switch between subjects at any time. Whether to do all of one subject first or interleave is personal preference.

**Most efficient pattern:** all of English first (it's compulsory and tends to have the highest "easy questions per minute" ratio), then your strongest subject, then weaker subjects last.

**Why English first:** the comprehension passages take time. Doing them when you're freshest costs less than doing them when tired. Also, English typically has the most "you either know it or you don't" questions — quick to answer.

## The Question Palette

Bottom of screen, each subject has a 1-40 grid showing question status:

- **Green** — answered
- **Red** — not yet answered
- **Yellow** — flagged for review (you marked it)
- **Grey** — not visited

Use this in the final 5-10 minutes to jump directly to red and yellow questions. It's much faster than clicking Next 40 times.

## Submitting

When you click "Finish Exam":

1. A confirmation dialog appears: "Are you sure?"
2. The dialog tells you how many questions are unanswered (e.g. "You have 3 unanswered questions")
3. **Always answer all 160 questions before submitting** — there's no negative marking, so a guess is worth more than a blank
4. Confirm submission

After submission:

- Your raw score appears immediately (each correct answer × 2.5 marks)
- The official JAMB result is released in May/June, typically 4-6 weeks after the exam window ends
- Don't take the immediate score as final — there are sometimes adjustments for cancelled/disqualified questions

## Common surprises

Things that catch first-time CBT candidates off-guard:

1. **The screen size**. JAMB CBT screens are often smaller than what you've practised on. The question text wraps differently. The first question feels weird; by question 5 you adjust.
2. **The mouse vs keyboard**. You can navigate with arrow keys or by clicking. Most students use mouse only — but keyboard is faster for repeated next/previous.
3. **The clock format**. The JAMB clock counts DOWN, not up. When it says 1:30:00 it means 1 hour 30 minutes left.
4. **Power outages**. If the centre's generator kicks in mid-exam, the system pauses and resumes from where you were. Your answers are saved server-side.
5. **The "Are you sure?" submit dialog feels final.** Because it is. Don't accidentally double-click.

## After you finish

- Walk out of the hall calmly
- Pick up your phone + bag from the storage area
- **Don't compare answers with other candidates outside.** Your score is already submitted; comparing answers will mostly stress you out about questions you actually got right.
- Get home. Sleep. Wait for the official result.
