WAEC SSCE 2026 papers run across 5-6 weeks, with multiple subjects most days. Unlike JAMB (one 2-hour CBT session), WAEC is paper-based, multi-paper per subject, and includes both objective and theory questions. That changes how you study.

> **Verify the official 2026 timetable on the WAEC Nigeria portal** before locking in your plan: [waecdirect.org](https://waecdirect.org/) for results, the WAEC Nigeria site for the timetable. Subject ordering and dates can shift each year.

## What a WAEC subject paper looks like

Most WAEC subjects have:

- **Paper 1**: 50 multiple-choice questions, 1 hour
- **Paper 2**: theory / essay / structured response, 2-3 hours

For English Language: Paper 1 (objective + lexis), Paper 2 (essay + comprehension), Paper 3 (oral, separate session).
For science subjects: Paper 1 (objective), Paper 2 (theory), Paper 3 (practical — alternative to practical for private candidates).

This means **you cannot just be good at multiple choice**. The theory papers carry more marks per minute and reward depth — clear definitions, named techniques, worked examples. WAEC examiners give marks for specific keywords in the marking scheme; missing the term often costs the mark even if your reasoning is correct.

## A realistic 6-week plan

If you're reading this 6+ weeks out, here's a workable schedule. Adjust the subjects for whatever you're actually sitting.

### Week 1-2: Audit + foundations

- Take one **objective practice paper per subject** to find your accuracy floor. Anything < 50% needs foundations work, not past papers.
- Spend 60% of your study time on the 2-3 weakest subjects.
- For science theory papers, write out the **list of techniques the syllabus expects you to know** (e.g. for Chemistry: titration calculation steps, mole concept, qualitative analysis tests). If you can't list them, start with the syllabus PDF.

### Week 3-4: Full past papers, marked honestly

- One full past paper per subject per week, under timed conditions.
- **Mark the theory papers against WAEC's published marking schemes** — don't be generous. WAEC awards marks for specific phrases; "kind of correct" gets you zero.
- Keep a "missed marks" log per subject — write down the technique or term that cost you each time.
- For English: do 2 essays per week minimum. The first you write properly; the second you grade against the marking scheme. Most students under-perform here because essays don't feel like "learning."

### Week 5: Theory drilling

- Drop the multiple-choice practice (you've done enough).
- For each subject, write 4-6 theory answers per week from past papers.
- For Math and Physics: practise SHOWING your working. WAEC awards method marks even when the final number is wrong; students lose them by writing only the answer.
- For English: master the article / argumentative essay structure. Most students don't know what an opening paragraph looks like in WAEC's marking scheme — open with a clear thesis, signpost the three points, then write them.

### Week 6: Light practice + sleep

The week before exam you should NOT be cramming. You should be:

- Doing 30-60 minutes of light practice per day to keep your hand in
- Sleeping 8 hours every night (research is unambiguous; sleep-deprived students lose 5-10% on exams)
- Eating consistent meals
- Reading through your "missed marks" log nightly so the techniques become automatic

## Subject-specific strategy

### English Language

The single highest-leverage subject because everyone takes it. Focus areas:

1. **Comprehension** (40% of Paper 2) — read the questions before reading the passage. This is roughly 2x faster.
2. **Summary writing** — pick 3-5 key points, write them in your own words, hit the word count.
3. **Essay writing** — pick the topic you can write 450 words on confidently, not the most interesting topic. Examiners mark thousands of essays; clarity wins over creativity.
4. **Lexis/structure** — drill 200+ multiple-choice items. The patterns repeat.
5. **Oral English** — vowel sounds, consonant sounds, stress patterns. Use ExamReady's [oral practice mode](/practice) — the audio matters.

### Mathematics

50 objective + 13 theory questions (you typically attempt 10 of 13).

- Algebra and number-and-numeration carry the most marks. If you're weak there, prioritise.
- For theory, **always show your formula before substituting numbers**. The marking scheme awards 1-2 marks just for stating the formula correctly.
- Trigonometry, statistics, and mensuration appear every year. Don't skip them.

### Biology, Chemistry, Physics

Theory questions are heavily formula- and definition-based. Practise:

- **Drawing labelled diagrams** — Biology especially. Stained drawings get partial credit; sketchy diagrams get nothing.
- **Working in units** — every numerical answer in Physics needs the unit. Forgetting it costs marks WAEC openly publishes in their schemes.
- **Stating principles before applying them** — "Newton's second law states F = ma. Substituting F = 50N and m = 10kg gives a = 5 m/s²" beats jumping straight to the calculation.

## What to do exam day

- Arrive at the centre 90 minutes early. WAEC denies entry to late candidates.
- Bring 2 black biros, mathematical sets, your candidate slip, your photo ID
- Read the cover page of every paper before starting — it tells you how many questions to attempt
- For theory: attempt the easy questions FIRST. Get your guaranteed marks before tackling harder ones.
- If you finish early, RE-READ. Most lost marks in theory come from wrong techniques on questions students could have answered, not from running out of time.
