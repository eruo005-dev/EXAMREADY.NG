NECO June 2026 runs the same way it has for years: paper-based, multi-paper per subject, 9 subjects max with 8-9 typically taken. If you're reading this in May with the exam in June, you've got about 30 days. Here's how to use them.

> **Verify the official 2026 timetable on the NECO Nigeria portal** before relying on dates: [mynecoexams.com](https://mynecoexams.com/). We don't reproduce the timetable here because it shifts year-to-year.

## NECO vs WAEC — what's actually different?

If you took our [WAEC vs NECO comparison](/blog/waec-vs-neco-2026), you know NECO is generally easier on Paper 1 (objective) and tougher on Paper 2 (theory) — though the gap has narrowed recently. Practical differences for studying:

- **NECO objective** repeats themes more aggressively than WAEC. Past-paper drilling is more effective.
- **NECO theory marking** has slightly more lenient mark allocation per criterion. You still need to name techniques, but partial credit is more generous.
- **NECO practical/alternative-to-practical** is structurally similar to WAEC. Same study approach.
- **NECO English oral** is paper-based (you read sentences and pick the answer); WAEC's is now audio in many states.

## The 30-day plan

Calibrate for the subjects you're sitting. Most NECO candidates take 8-9 subjects; the framework below is per-subject so you'll multiply.

### Day 1-3: Diagnostic week

- Take ONE past objective paper per subject under timed conditions
- Mark honestly. Anything < 50% goes to the "weak" pile.
- Anything ≥ 75% goes to "maintenance" — don't waste hours on subjects you've already got.

Allocate study time: 70% on weak subjects, 30% on maintenance.

### Day 4-15: Past papers + theory drilling

This is the meat of preparation.

- **3 days per weak subject** at this stage. Do one past paper per session — objective + theory together.
- For theory: write the answer, THEN check the marking scheme, THEN rewrite the answer to fix the missing techniques/terms.
- For maintenance subjects: 1 hour every 3 days, just to keep them active.

Example day: 3 hours on Math (weak) → 1 hour on English Language theory → 30 mins of Government objective drill.

### Day 16-22: Targeted gap-filling

By now you should have a clear picture of what you're getting wrong. The pattern is usually:

- 1-2 specific topics per subject keep coming back wrong
- 1-2 question types (e.g. "calculate from a graph", "translate the idiom") cost you most of your missed marks

Spend this week on those specific gaps, not on broad practice.

### Day 23-28: Full mock exams

- One **full** subject paper per day (objective + theory together, under exam conditions)
- Mark hard. Note every missed mark.
- The most useful part of a mock is the next morning when you sit down and ask "why did I miss those specific marks?" — answer that, you've improved.

### Day 29-30: Light practice + sleep

Same as our [WAEC last-minute guide](/blog/waec-ssce-2026-timetable) §"Week 6: Light practice + sleep". Same logic applies for NECO.

## Subject-specific notes

### English Language

NECO English has the same comprehension + lexis + summary + essay structure. Same priorities as WAEC English (see /blog/waec-ssce-2026-timetable). The lexis section in NECO leans on Nigerian-context vocabulary slightly more than WAEC; if you're a strong reader of Nigerian newspapers and novels, you'll have an edge.

### Mathematics

NECO Mathematics theory is structured similarly to WAEC's: 13 theory questions, attempt 10. The objective is 50 questions in 1 hour 30 minutes (longer than WAEC's 1 hour). Use the extra time — NECO Math objective rewards careful working over speed.

### Biology, Chemistry, Physics

Theory questions in NECO sciences ask for slightly more **practical-knowledge** answers than WAEC. Examples:

- "Describe the procedure for separating salt from sea water" — NECO will want the actual lab steps + equipment, not just the principle.
- "Outline the test for hydrogen gas" — NECO expects "lit splint produces a 'pop' sound", not just "burns".

Practise the practical-procedure questions. The Alternative to Practical paper specifically tests this.

### Government, History, CRK / IRK

NECO essay marking is more keyword-driven than WAEC. Each marking-scheme point typically wants a SPECIFIC term. If the scheme says "the principle of separation of powers", writing "the three branches don't overlap" gets you partial credit at best.

For History and Government especially: memorise the **named** principles, dates, and people. NECO awards marks for naming specific historical actors and acts; vague "the British colonised Nigeria" beats no answer but costs you most of the marks.
